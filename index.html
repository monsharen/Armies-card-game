Four Banners

♥ ♠ ♦ ♣

"Banners" for short — four armies, one city, one regular deck of cards.

October 1813. Napoleon's Grande Armée has fallen back on Leipzig, and every road to the city fills with soldiers — the coalition's armies of Bohemia, Silesia, Poland and the North converging from all points of the compass. History remembers it as the Battle of the Nations, the greatest battle Europe would see until the World Wars. Four Banners deals that autumn onto your table: four armies, one per suit, marching down four roads to take Leipzig — and hold it.

🃏

Just a deck of cards

Each suit is an army: number cards are soldiers, Aces are champions, Jacks raid, Queens are banners, Kings are generals. No components to buy — the whole game is one standard 52-card deck, and the cards themselves are the units on the board.

🏰

Build your host

Stack up to three cards into a single army — its strength is their sum. Heavy hosts hit hard but march slowly; lone scouts race ahead. Merge your columns at the gate and storm Leipzig, which pays its holder tribute every turn.

🎲

Always four armies

Play with 1–4 players — any army without a player runs on a deterministic flip-two-cards script. Two humans? The other two armies march themselves.

✏️

Pen-and-paper ready

Everything here works on a kitchen table: the board is nine boxes and a cross you can sketch in a minute, the cards are the units, and the only bookkeeping is a glory tally. Print the reference sheet and go.
