How to Play

Four Armies is a war game for one standard 52-card deck. Four armies — ♥ Crimson Host, ♠ Black Legion, ♦ Golden Company and ♣ Green Banner — race down their roads to capture the central Citadel and hold it through two seasons of war. 1–4 players; every army without a player runs itself with a simple scripted turn.

The board

A Citadel in the middle; four roads of 3 spaces radiating out to each army's camp. Cards are the units: a road space holds at most one card, the camp holds any number. (On a real table, sketch the cross on paper or just lay cards in rows — see Print & Play.)

What your cards mean

Cards of your own suit are your army:

Cards of any other suit are supply — you discard one to pay for each march. About three-quarters of what you draw is supply, so soldiers are precious and movement is cheap.

Setup

Shuffle the deck. Deal 4 cards to each human player (automated armies hold no hand). Flip one card onto the Citadel — that's the neutral mercenary garrison, defending at its printed strength (J=11, Q=12, K=13, A=11). Turn order runs ♥ → ♠ → ♦ → ♣; with two players you sit at ♥ and ♦, so play alternates human and automated army.

A player turn

1. Draw 2 cards (hand limit 7 — discard down after drawing).
2. Take up to 2 actions, any mix of:

ActionWhat happens
🛡️ DeployPlay a card of your suit: soldiers, Champion and Jack go to your camp; Queen and King take their camp posts.
🥾 MarchDiscard any off-suit card; one of your units advances 1 space (up to 2 with your King). Camp → road, road → road, or gate → assault the Citadel. No jumping over occupied spaces.
🗡️ RaidPlay your Jack at any enemy unit on any road. Fight at 11 (+2 with your Banner). Win or lose, the Jack is discarded — raiders always withdraw.

You may also hold position (end your turn early).

Battles

One comparison: attacker strength vs. defender strength, each +2 if their Queen is in camp. Defender wins ties. The loser's card goes to the discard pile. A unit that wins an assault enters the Citadel and becomes the new garrison; its army scores +5 glory. Winning any other battle — a raid, or defending — scores +1 glory.

Automated armies

An automated army's turn is two card flips off the deck, resolved one at a time — any player can run it in seconds:

FlipThe army…
Its own suitDeploys it: soldiers/Ace to camp; Q and K take their posts; a Jack raids immediately — the enemy unit nearest the Citadel (tie: strongest, then clockwise) — or joins the camp if there's nothing to raid.
Any other suitMarches: its frontmost unit advances as far as it can (assaulting the Citadel from the gate); if no road unit can move, its strongest camp unit steps out; with no units at all, the supply is wasted.

No decisions, fully deterministic — and relentless: automated armies always come down the road at you.

Seasons, glory and winning

When the deck runs out, the season ends: whoever holds the Citadel scores +3 glory, the discard pile is reshuffled, and a second season is played. When the deck runs out again, the war is over — most glory wins.

DeedGlory
Capture the Citadel+5 🏅
Hold the Citadel at season's end+3 🏅
Win a raid or a defense+1 🏅

Strategy tips

👑 Get your court out early. The Queen's +2 swings almost every battle, and the King's double marches nearly halve your supply costs. They're worth an action the moment you draw them.
🏰 The Citadel compounds. +5 to take it, +3 per season to hold it, +1 for every assault you repel — and your Queen's +2 defends it. Garrison something big.
🗡️ Save the Jack for a tempo strike. Raiding the unit at an enemy's gate just before their assault wastes several of their turns. Raiding a 3 by the camp wastes yours.
🥾 Small soldiers screen. A 2 at your gate can't take the Citadel — but as raid bait and a road-blocker it buys your real column time.
