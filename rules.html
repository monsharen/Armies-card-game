How to Play

Far from any map lies Kartenburg, the castle of cards, which pays tribute to whoever holds its walls. Each season the four realms of the deck march on its gates; mercenaries man the ramparts until someone takes them.

Four Banners is a war game for one standard 52-card deck. Four armies — ♥ Hearts, ♠ Spades, ♦ Diamonds and ♣ Clover — build fighting hosts out of their suit's cards and march them down their roads to capture Kartenburg, which pays its holder tribute every turn. 1–4 players; every army without a player runs itself with a simple scripted turn.

The board

Kartenburg in the middle; four roads of 3 spaces radiating out to each army's camp. A road space holds at most one army; the camp holds any number. (On a real table, sketch the cross on paper or just lay cards in rows — see Print & Play.)

Armies are stacks

A unit on the board is an army: a stack of up to 3 cards of your suit. Its strength is the sum of its cards (+2 while your Queen's banner flies). Building the best army — and choosing when it's strong enough — is the whole game.

Cards of any other suit are supply: marching an army costs one supply per card in its stack. Big hosts hit hard but move slowly and expensively; a lone scout is fast and cheap.

Setup

Shuffle the deck. Deal 4 cards to each human player (automated armies hold no hand). Flip two cards onto Kartenburg — the neutral mercenary garrison, defending at their combined strength. Turn order runs ♥ → ♠ → ♦ → ♣; with two players you sit at ♥ and ♦, so play alternates human and automated army.

A player turn

0. Tribute: if your army holds Kartenburg, +1 glory.
1. Draw 2 cards (hand limit 7 — discard down after drawing).
2. Take up to 2 actions, any mix of:

ActionWhat happens
🛡️ DeployPlay a card of your suit: found a new army in camp, or reinforce any of your armies — in camp, on the road, or garrisoning Kartenburg (max 3 cards). Queen and King take their camp posts instead.
🥾 MarchDiscard one supply per card in the stack (King: one less, min 1); the army advances one space. Marching onto your own army merges them (if the result fits under 3 cards); marching from the gate assaults Kartenburg.
🗡️ RaidPlay your Jack at any enemy army on a road. It strikes the army's weakest card, fighting at 11 (+2 with your Banner). Win and that card is discarded. Win or lose, the Jack withdraws (discard).

You may also hold position (end your turn early).

Battles

One comparison: attacker's stack total vs. defender's stack total, each +2 if their Queen is in camp. Defender wins ties. The losing army is destroyed (all its cards discarded) — and the winner discards its weakest card as casualties (a single-card army is spared). Victories cost blood: even the mightiest host erodes with every battle it wins, garrisons included.

A victorious assault moves the attacking army (after casualties) into Kartenburg as the new garrison: +5 glory. Repelling an assault or winning either side of a raid: +1 glory.

Kartenburg pays tribute 👑

At the start of each of your turns, if your army garrisons Kartenburg you collect +1 glory. This is the game's clock: every turn you spend at home assembling the perfect host, the current holder banks another point. Garrisons can be reinforced like any army — but they erode by one card each time they repel an assault, so even a fortress can be brought down by waves of cheap attackers.

Automated armies

An automated army's turn is two card flips off the deck, resolved one at a time — any player can run it in seconds:

FlipThe army…
Its own suitQ and K take their posts. A Jack raids the enemy army nearest Kartenburg (tie: strongest, then clockwise) — or reinforces if there's nothing to raid. Anything else reinforces the most forward army with room (garrison first, then road, then camp), or founds a new army.
Any other suitGoes to its supply pile (face up beside its road). The moment the pile covers the frontmost army's march cost, that army marches (assaulting from the gate) and the pile is spent.

No decisions, fully deterministic — automated hosts mass at the gate and surge whenever their supplies allow.

When the deck runs out, the season ends: the discard pile is reshuffled and a second season is played. When the deck runs out again the war is over — most glory wins.

Strategy tips

⚖️ Strong enough beats perfect. The tribute clock means a 2-card army that takes Kartenburg today usually out-earns a 3-card monster that arrives four turns later. Count the garrison's strength and build one point past it — no more.
🌊 Fortresses fall to waves. A repelled assault still knocks the weakest card out of the garrison. Two cheap sacrificial attacks can strip a 30-strength garrison down to one card — then your real army walks in.
🗡️ Raid the siege camp. A Jack that picks the 10 out of an enemy's gate army wastes several of their turns and supplies. Save raids for stacks about to assault.
🧩 Assemble at the gate. Two small armies marching separately are cheaper to move than one big one — merge them at the last road space, strike, and let your King make the whole operation almost free.
